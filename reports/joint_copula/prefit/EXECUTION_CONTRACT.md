## Numerical reasoning

With shared fixed t8 marginals, their inverse-CDF composition is the identity.
For the Gaussian copula, compute the smaller t8 tail in log space and invert the
normal log CDF; this avoids artificial tail observations from clipping a rounded
CDF endpoint. The [SciPy inverse-log-CDF documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.ndtri_exp.html)
describes that inverse. Scaled positive quadratic forms prevent avoidable
overflow in the fixed-t joint density.

For t8, set `d=1-rho^2` and
`A=8*d+z1^2+z2^2-2*rho*z1*z2`. Up to a rho-independent constant, negative log
copula likelihood is `5*mean(log(A))-4.5*log(d)`. On a closed interval,
concavity of each A means its minimum is attained at an endpoint. This supplies
a lower Hessian bound and therefore a valid midpoint Taylor lower bound.
All final disjoint intervals are saved for a separate implementation to check
full-domain coverage and the numerical optimum gap. The Gaussian optimum uses
all admissible real stationary cubic roots and both boundary points; a local
optimizer success flag alone cannot establish either optimum.

Generated tests check density ratios against independent distributions,
marginal preservation by integration, variance scaling, log-score cancellation,
zero-correlation t8 dependence, symmetry, extreme tails, multiple stationary
points, boundary solutions, whole-domain bounds, parameter restrictions,
clock gaps, output mutation and whole-family failure retention.

The dependence construction follows established copula mathematics; see
[Embrechts, Lindskog and McNeil, Modelling Dependence with Copulas](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~embrecht/ftp/copchapter.pdf).
The distinct question here is whether it improves this repo's specified joint
forecast with the same marginal assumptions. It is not a claim of literature
novelty, pure tail-dependence identification or a new data source.
