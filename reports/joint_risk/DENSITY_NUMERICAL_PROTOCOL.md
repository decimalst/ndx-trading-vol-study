# Matrix-score numerical protocol and synthetic evidence

Fixed on 2026-09-07 before empirical measurement construction or fitting for this wave. This component read no empirical observations, forecasts or scores. Earlier studies and their Python files were not changed.

The public module is `src/joint_risk_density.py`. Its primary score is the unhalved value `logdet(H) + e' inv(H) e`. `matrix_score(residuals, h, rho)` returns one score per two-asset residual row, where `H = diag(sqrt(h)) R(rho) diag(sqrt(h))`; `full_matrix_score(residuals, covariance)` accepts an exactly symmetric common or row-specific positive-definite matrix. Finite positive diagonals and strict `abs(rho)<1` are required. Zero residuals and rank-one realized outer products are valid. No determinant or inverse of the realized target is used. Nonfinite or unrepresentable scores fail without clipping, jitter, or variance repair. The implementation avoids forming the product of marginal variances, so extreme but representable changes of units remain usable. Asset permutation leaves the score unchanged; a units change adds only the corresponding constant log-determinant shift.

`fit_dependence(standardized_residuals)` estimates a constant intercept `a0`. `fit_dependence(standardized_residuals, z, constant=a0)` estimates only a slope `b`, holding that intercept fixed. In both cases `rho = 0.995*tanh(eta)`, with `eta=a0` or `eta=a0+b*z`. Both scalar parameter bounds are `[-4,4]`. The slope objective adds `0.01*b**2` to the mean correlation-dependent score. The constant intercept has no penalty. Signal standardization is the caller's responsibility and must use training observations only. Both fits expose their objective, analytic derivative and bound-aware projected gradient. The correlation matrix has minimum eigenvalue at least 0.005 even when the transform saturates in floating arithmetic.

For standardized residuals `(u,v)`, put `A=mean(u²+v²)` and `B=mean(u*v)`. The constant objective's stationary correlations solve `rho³ - B*rho² + (A-1)*rho - B = 0`. The implementation compares all real bounded roots and both endpoints. The real-root imaginary-part allowance and relative polynomial-residual check are each `1e-10`. Objective ties within `1e-12` choose the smaller parameter. Nonconvex objectives, including sign-symmetric minima caused by misspecified marginal scales, therefore do not rely on one local start.

The candidate uses a deterministic numerical branch-and-bound value certificate. A fixed L-BFGS-B refinement starts at zero and at each subsequently improved interval midpoint; settings are `maxiter=1000`, `maxls=50`, `ftol=1e-14`, and `gtol=1e-9`. Every attempt and solver status is retained. Solver success alone never certifies global optimality. Only coefficients satisfying projected-gradient magnitude at most `1e-7` can become incumbents. Failed local explorations do not remove intervals. The final acceptance requires a valid incumbent and a finite global value gap between zero and `1e-8`, inclusive. An inconsistent bound or exhaustion of 32,768 interval splits fails explicitly.

For an interval midpoint `m` and radius `d`, the lower bound is based on `f(m)-abs(f'(m))*d-0.5*L*d²`. For each residual row, let `P=rho³-B_i*rho²+(A_i-1)*rho-B_i`, so `g_r=2P/(1-rho²)²` and `g_rr=2P'/(1-rho²)²+8*rho*P/(1-rho²)³`. Endpoint correlation ranges bound the absolute values of these derivatives. The maximum first and second derivatives of the tanh transform are bounded over the full corresponding signal interval, including its interior second-derivative extrema. Combining these row bounds and adding the penalty curvature gives `L`.

The fixed floating inflation is `eps_mult=256*machine_epsilon`. The curvature bound becomes `nextafter(L*(1+eps_mult)+eps_mult,+inf)`. With `spread=abs(f'(m))*d+0.5*L*d²`, the saved interval bound is `nextafter(f(m)-spread-eps_mult*(1+abs(f(m))+spread),-inf)`. The incumbent upper bound is `f(best)+eps_mult*(1+abs(f(best)))`. The constant certificate similarly inflates the lowest root/endpoint objective. This is a numerical value certificate with conservative rounding allowances, not a proof using exact interval arithmetic. Every retained leaf and lower bound is saved; the leaves form a contiguous partition of the entire parameter box. An independent verifier can recompute all bounds without matching the search trajectory.

All audits include `parameter`, `objective`, `gradient`, `projected_gradient`, `bounds`, `rho_max`, `penalty`, `constant`, both tolerance fields, `global_lower_bound`, `global_upper_bound`, `global_value_gap`, `interval_splits`, `function_evaluations`, `local_attempts`, and `success`. The constant audit additionally includes all cubic roots and evaluated candidates. The slope audit additionally includes `maximum_interval_splits`, `local_options`, `floating_inflation`, and `certificate_intervals` containing `left`, `right`, and `lower_bound`. Each local attempt records its start, final parameter, objective, raw/projected gradient, success, status, message, iterations and function evaluations.

The tests were written before the initial implementation; the missing-module failure remains in `density_initial_red.log`. Eighteen final synthetic tests pass in `density_final_synthetic_checks.log`. They cover independent Cholesky score calculations, units and ordering, zero/signed residuals, invalid or unrepresentable inputs, analytic derivatives, exact nesting and mean-loss scaling, cubic recovery and boundary/nonconvex optima, curvature and lower-envelope checks, full leaf coverage, recovery of a known positive slope, and explicit certificate-budget failure. Independent review found that a negative certificate gap lacked an explicit rejection; prewritten fault injections reproduced candidate lower-above-upper and constant negative/NaN gaps in `density_negative_gap_initial_red.log`, then passed after the guard was added before freezing. No acceptance tolerance was relaxed.

A separate fixed-seed synthetic runtime check uses seed 20260916, sample sizes 1,000 and 4,000, and three scenarios: high correlation, misspecified marginal scales, and a standardized signal with an outlying observation. All six constant-plus-slope fits certified. The final run used at most 195 splits and 0.146 seconds per pair with one BLAS thread. Its exact settings, outcomes, package versions and file hashes are recorded in `density_large_synthetic_final.log`. These checks assess numerical behavior and cost; they are not evidence of predictive performance.

The two-state counterexample deliberately has true conditional correlation zero in both states but incorrect supplied diagonal second moments. A changing correlation forecast then improves the matrix objective. Therefore a later score improvement can support conditional joint residual second-moment prediction, but cannot by itself establish discovery of changing correlation independently of marginal model misspecification.
