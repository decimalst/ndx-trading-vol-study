OVX and GVZ: official-source feasibility review

September 8, 2026. **Both are feasible candidates for bounded source acquisition; neither is yet an admitted point-in-time dataset or a demonstrated predictive signal.** Cboe publishes identifiable historical-download links, documents both indices as launched in 2008, and provides methodology and change notices. A prospective common source start of **2009-01-02**, with the existing **2025-10-20** ceiling, avoids reliance on prelaunch history. No new hypothesis was registered by this review; the completed-study count remains 142 comparisons.

The proposed information channel is commodity ETF option-implied uncertainty. It differs from claims releases and from a further transformation of equity closing prices. A plausible hypothesis is that oil-related uncertainty or gold-related hedging demand contains information about subsequent equity volatility beyond the existing equity-IV and cross-asset-return controls. This is an interpretation to test, not a finding about lead-lag relationships or causality. The existing baseline already uses USO and GLD returns within cross-asset stress; that overlap must remain a control rather than being described as entirely new commodity information. Feature, horizon and comparison choices belong to the separately frozen prospective design.

The underlying-option identities appear in Cboe's [2011 volatility-index options filing, page 17](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/regulation/rule_filings/other/options_filings/SR-CBOE-2011-026.a1.pdf) and [GVZ futures filing, page 4](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/regulation/rule_filings/approved/2011/SR-CFE-2011-002.pdf). The latter explicitly distinguishes GVZ's August 1, 2008 introduction from its later derivative-contract listing and states that real-time dissemination had occurred each trading day since introduction. Cboe's [2008 annual report, page 11](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/annual_reports/annualreport2008.pdf) confirms that OVX, GVZ and EVZ were introduced during 2008. The GVZ filing's indexed official-document text was available; a subsequent direct web-open attempt returned a transient retrieval error. That limitation is retained rather than claiming a newly hash-authenticated copy.

Both indices target **30 calendar days**. Current rules use nearby monthly PM-settled options expiring on the third Friday, exclude terms with fewer than seven days remaining, and source quotes from NBBO. Their nearest-term construction differs from assuming every VIX-family index uses the same weekly-option bracket. Current RTH dissemination is approximately every 15 seconds, **9:31 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Eastern**, subject to shortened sessions. Index-level filtering can suppress transient changes; when calculation is unavailable, the last disseminated value can be republished. See the [selected equity/ETF volatility methodology, sections 2–5](https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/governance/Volatility_Index_Methodology_Selected_Broad_Based_Index_Equity_and_ETF_Volatility_Indices.pdf).

The indices apply the VIX-style option-strip variance calculation and term weighting, with option bid/ask midpoints and calendar-year time scaling. The published index is **annualized volatility in percentage-point units**, not dollar prices, realized variance, or a 30-day cumulative percentage return. This follows the annual time denominator and square-root-times-100 formula in [Cboe's volatility mathematics methodology, sections 3–4](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/Cboe_Volatility_Index_Mathematics_Methodology.pdf). USO/GLD option-implied risk should therefore be described as ETF risk; it is not a direct observation of spot oil/gold volatility or an unbiased physical expectation.

Two dated methodology changes require explicit treatment:

- **2022-06-06, before the open:** Cboe's [April 8 consultation](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/release_notes/2022/EQ-ETF-Volatility-Indices-Consultation.pdf) specifically proposed changing GVZ and OVX pricing from Cboe C1 quotes to NBBO. Its [May 13 update, reference C2022051801](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/release_notes/2022/Update-EQ-ETF-Volatility-Indices-Consultation-Summary.pdf), confirms implementation before the June 6 open. The consultation's weekly-option changes for other indices must not be attributed to OVX/GVZ automatically.
- **2025-02-10:** the [January 27 notice, reference C2025013102](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/release_notes/2025/Modifications-to-the-Strike-Selection-in-Volatility-Index-Calculations.pdf), names both indices and changes strike exclusion from zero bid to **zero bid or zero ask**, including the consecutive-zero stopping rule. This is inside the proposed historical scope.

The current selected-index PDF is version 9.0, revised March 30, 2026, and remains a current mechanism description. It lists both OVX and GVZ; no cessation notice for either was found in this bounded review. Neither inclusion nor a historical download link proves uninterrupted daily calculation. The current mathematics PDF is version 5.0, revised February 26, 2026. These current documents cannot establish which historical bytes or every historical rule were available at each earlier close; the dated change notices are stronger evidence for the specified transition dates.

Revision risk is concrete. [Cboe Index Policies & Practices, section 3.3 and Appendix 2](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/governance/Cboe_Index_Policies_Practices.pdf) allows retroactive recalculation and file restatement. Its derivative-index guidance includes reviewing incorrect inputs or end-of-day levels identified within two business days, and generating missing end-of-day values when an issue is resolved. Intraday corrections need not be reconstructed. The appendix was introduced April 9, 2025; the currently served policy was revised in March 2026, so it is not evidence that this exact policy governed the full sample. The public materials reviewed do not establish an immutable, freely available archive of original daily CSV vintages.

For source timing, next-observed-session use is a defensible **prospective conservative convention** relative to the documented close, but it does not authenticate that today's historical CSV value was the value then published. The exact CSV update time, late-publication handling, prior-year revision/recalculation scope and date-specific historical vintages remain unverified. A one-session lag cannot repair retrospective source revisions by itself. The regime changes must be disclosed, and the public historical files must not be silently recomputed under the current formula.

The bounded next step is therefore root-owned quarantine acquisition of the two exact official files, followed by header/date-scope inspection, saved transport and byte hashes, and prewritten parser contracts before numerical admission. The parser should reject post-ceiling dates before converting their value fields, preserve the full bounded calendar and missingness, and distinguish any pre-2009 metadata from eligible observations. Acquisition must not be reported as source admission; immutable historical availability remains a limitation unless separately authenticated.
