# Prospective calendar-channel audit

**Unregistered; separate from wave 13.** This bounded review reads existing source and protocol definitions. It does not construct calendar features, binary labels, support counts, associations, losses, or forecasts, and does not inspect wave 13 outcomes or sealed numerical data. Existing code, protocols, and reports remain unchanged.

The remaining defensible calendar question is narrow: **does a fixed civil quarter-end interaction improve a one-session SPX risk forecast after ordinary month-end, annual seasonality, year-end, and the existing market/macro controls are available?** Calendar prediction itself is already extensively represented in this repository. No index-rebalance or options-expiry mechanism is established by this audit.

## One precisely known calendar increment

For each origin civil date `t`, let `n(t)` be the first **Monday-through-Friday civil date strictly after t**. Determine it from Gregorian dates alone, skipping weekends only. Do not look up the next observed price row, future holidays, or an exchange schedule. This matches the existing calendar-variance nominal endpoint convention; the actual next traded session may differ.

For that nominal date, define `E5=1` when it lies within the final five **civil dates** of its month, including month end. Define `Y5=E5*1[month=December]` and the single candidate

`G5=E5*1[month is March, June, or September]`.

This is a fixed interaction for the **first three calendar quarter-ends**, with December deliberately assigned to a separate year-end control. It is not the last five exchange sessions, an expiration week, or a rebalance window. The five-date width and exclusion of December are prospective choices, not estimates from inspected outcomes. No alternative width or month subset should be searched within this family.

All these values, and nominal month-of-year indicators, are computable before origin `t` closes even if no future market observations are supplied. Leap years, month lengths, weekends, and year rollover follow civil arithmetic; holidays and early closes are deliberately ignored. The feature can consequently be misaligned with the next actual market session near a closure. That is a disclosed proxy limitation, not a reason to replace the nominal date retrospectively.

## Minimal strong-control comparison

Use the existing matched SPX target from [calendar variance](/Users/byrons/code/trading-vol/ndx-vol-experiment/calendar_variance.yaml): next observed session `max(GK,1e-10)+log(next open/current close)^2`, in native squared-log-return units. This is an archival daily OHLC risk proxy, not measured high-frequency integrated variance. Using SPX avoids making an unverified QQQ distribution-date adjustment part of the new calendar claim.

The baseline should retain the entire original 18-feature SPX design: own risk means and downside histories over 1/5/22 sessions, VIX/short-IV shape/VVIX, entry weekdays, nominal hours, and all three admitted original macro-plan flags. Add eleven nominal month-of-year indicators, `E5`, and `Y5` as nuisance controls. Thus the candidate cannot earn novelty merely from ordinary month-end, December year-end, or additive annual seasonality. `G5` is an interaction not generally in the linear span of those main effects; no raw feature merely duplicating an existing linear combination should be added.

A minimal candidate freezes the baseline's fitted positive risk function and adds one training-centered `G5` slope in its log prediction. Fit that slope using the same mean proper variance loss plus a fixed ridge penalty, with no additional calibration intercept or baseline refit. Its zero slope exactly reproduces the baseline. Predeclare centering, scaling, finite arithmetic, constant-input handling, and optimizer/certificate tolerances before construction. The objective is a conditional seasonality increment, not a claim to identify a structural quarter-end mechanism.

Register only two primary comparisons: candidate versus that strong baseline, and candidate versus the historical mean target on exactly the same mature training rows. Score every retained origin with `log(h)+y/h`; do not select quarter-end observations as the primary sample. A fixed absolute decrease such as the existing `0.005` natural-log score threshold can be specified prospectively, rather than a percentage of a potentially negative raw score. Both comparisons must satisfy both phases, the fixed later-period signs, and newly allocated wave/cumulative multiplicity gates. No individual month, expiry, year-end, return, or event-slice claim is included in those two hypotheses.

## Feasibility and the next decision

Civil features require no acquired source or historical release timestamp. The existing audited SPX/Cboe inputs and original-plan corpus supply the other inputs, but their archival-vintage, plan-revision, nominal-window, and missing-month limitations remain. Preserve their strict common source-known-month mask; do not remove macro controls or zero-fill missing plans to recover calendar observations. This review has not recomputed whether the expanded design has sufficient complete or nonconstant support at every monthly fit.

Before any features, counts, or scores, freeze the exact new protocol, at least 1,000 mature training observations, full-reference rolling/maturity rules, minimum calendar-group support, new family/alpha/seed, and whole-wave failure treatment. Keep the existing 2016–2019 development boundary, 2020–2025 evaluation boundary and fixed slices, source/target fence at 2025-10-20, and sealed period from 2025-11-03. A date/source-only support audit may then establish feasibility without changing those rules. Prewritten tests should cover month/year/leap-day/weekend edges, nominal holidays remaining nominal, future-calendar mutation invariance, distinct main effects versus interaction, training-only transforms, source-known-month admission, label maturity, same-row controls, proper-loss arithmetic, and failure accounting.

An options-expiry or index-rebalance alternative would first require official product-specific rules and historical effective/announcement evidence, including holidays, exceptions, and rule changes. None was acquired or verified here, so no “third Friday,” rebalance schedule, or institutional-flow explanation is asserted. The civil interaction is the only bounded next candidate recommended by this audit, and remains prospective and unregistered.
