## One target and one candidate

Use the existing SPX raw OHLC calendar and `r_t = log(C[t+1]/C[t])`. Before entry close `t`, define a fixed, observable normalization:

`u_t = (r_t − trailing_22_mean(raw close log returns through t−1)) / sqrt(trailing_22_mean(GK-plus-raw-overnight variance through t−1))`.

Retain the already declared GK floor and strict missing-session windows. The binary target is **`u_t < −1.5`**, at one session only. This is a return measured against an ex ante trailing risk proxy; it is not an oracle-standardized innovation or a universal 5% VaR event. The target rule is independent of the fitted candidate, so its historical frequency is well defined without reconstructing a new sequence of past model forecasts.

Fit a heavy-tail conditional density for `u`, with location `mu(X)`, positive scale `sigma(X)`, constant degrees of freedom, and constant asymmetry. The baseline must contain the established own return/risk histories, downside-return histories, IV level/shape, weekdays, **and lagged SKEW plus its separate curvature in both location and scale**. Thus SKEW has an opportunity to explain mean or variance before the shape claim is tested. Freeze the exact feature basis and regularization before fitting.

Use **Hansen's moment-standardized skewed Student distribution**: its asymmetry parameter changes shape while its innovations retain mean zero and variance one. The official implementation documents separate tail-thickness and skewness parameters and a conditional variance interpretation. [Hansen (1994), *Autoregressive Conditional Density Estimation*](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~bhansen/papers/ier_94.pdf), [official `arch` distribution documentation](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/univariate/generated/arch.univariate.SkewStudent.html). The construction's internal centering and rescaling constants depend on both parameters and must be recomputed whenever asymmetry changes; only the external conditional location and scale stay fixed. The [official implementation's likelihood, constants, CDF, and moment routines](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/arch/univariate/distribution.html#SkewStudent) provide an independent formula reference for the future tests. No local normalization or density estimator has been implemented or verified for this proposal.

At each monthly fit, estimate the baseline using completed training labels. Then **freeze its fitted location, scale, and degrees of freedom for both forecasts**. The sole candidate replaces constant asymmetry with `lambda_t = 0.95 × tanh(a + b × S_t)`, where `S_t` is the training-standardized prior-session SKEW level. The control uses the same bound with `b=0`. The candidate introduces one state-dependent shape slope, not another location/scale fit. Its training residuals belong to the current training fit; do not describe them as errors forecast out of sample.

Both forecasts consequently have exactly the same predicted mean and variance. Tail probabilities come from their CDF at `(-1.5−mu_t)/sigma_t`. A fit that merely improves variance cannot by itself satisfy this matched comparison. Remaining location/scale misspecification can still masquerade as shape within an imperfect parametric family, so success would support this conditional specification, not prove a structural physical-skewness mechanism.
