## Boundary, freshness and label rules that must be frozen

1. **Choose a deterministic cutoff observation.** Specify event and receipt inequalities, eligible message types, quality flags, tie-breaking by sequence, maximum age and behavior when timestamp precision cannot order a boundary observation. A later tick must never backfill a missing 09:35 input. Choose any permitted age from documented feed semantics before inspecting values, not from a successful coverage fraction.

2. **Distinguish first and last observations of a minute.** An end-labeled `09:35` bar representing `[09:34,09:35)` may still be unavailable until after 09:35. Its close also predates the boundary unless the source establishes otherwise. A start-labeled `09:35` bar representing `[09:35,09:36)` contains future observations: its high/low/close are not cutoff inputs, and its first observation may occur after the decision. Neither label convention alone supplies an eligible `P_t`.

3. **Do not hide a stale starting mark inside “strictly remaining risk.”** A last eligible mark before 09:35 can be a valid observable predictor, but using it as the target's open can incorporate movement between that mark and 09:35. If it lies outside the future high/low, extending those extrema to include it changes the measured path. The source must support a precise boundary-state interpretation. Otherwise distinguish event-time post-cutoff risk from future changes in the observable feed's carried-forward mark. Changing to a receipt-time process, a later first print or another decision time requires a separately documented prospective estimand; it does not solve the original gate silently.

4. **Separate the response from the known shock.** For an admitted boundary state, the post-cutoff interval uses that state as its reference and only subsequent admissible observations through the declared session close. Specify endpoint inclusivity once. Do not use full-day extrema or add the prior-close-to-cutoff shock square. “GK” here is a functional of the admitted index-mark sampling process, not proof of integrated variance or executable portfolio risk.

5. **Freeze target publication maturity.** The current session's label becomes usable only when its required closing observations and chosen label vintage are available. A close timestamp with next-morning delivery after 09:35 cannot enter that morning's training or rolling reference. Late corrections must not rewrite earlier training information. The prior close used in the shock requires its own eligible vintage.

6. **Freeze the calendar treatment.** A 09:35-to-scheduled-close interval is shorter on an early-close day. If such days remain, define the literal preceding22 interval history and any calendar-known duration/session-class controls prospectively; duration differences otherwise become an additional information distinction. An alternative fixed calendar-only exclusion would change the proposed sample and must be declared before measurement. An unexpected later closure cannot retroactively remove an already issued application. Freeze whether the resulting label is valid, partial or unknown, and when that fact is available.

7. **Keep the full reference calendar and missingness.** Missing boundary input prevents that application; a missing eventual label does not erase an issued application. Missing historical intervals stay unknown rather than compressing a 22-session window. Decide zero ranges, nonpositive reference risk, revisions, invalid OHLC and unrepresentable arithmetic before parsing prices. Do not silently carry the older full-day GK floor into this new interval functional. Include the calendar and source-availability requirements in the independent reconstruction.

## The unresolved denominator changes the information claim

Write `q_t = log(P_t/C_(t−1))²` and `f_t = log1p(q_t/K_t)`. Even with `q_t` fixed at a nonzero constant, `f_t` varies nonlinearly with the new post-cutoff history `K_t`. A baseline containing only the old full-day columns can therefore lose because it lacks this history. Adding only a linear `log(K_t)` term does not represent the entire family `log1p(q/K_t)` for varying `q`. Training-centering the candidate or freezing the baseline logit does not remove that distinction. Residualization against a finite nuisance basis establishes neither general conditional independence nor causal isolation.

There are two prospectively defensible directions; **neither is selected or registered here**:

- **Retain the exact proposed ratio and bound the claim.** Predeclare the matched prior-interval history terms and their nonlinear geometry before numerical inspection, use them in the shared baseline, and describe the result as the incremental value of a specified joint current-shock/history transformation relative to that finite model. Explicitly deny a general isolated-current-information or causal claim. This is a revision of the frozen note's intended interpretation, not a declaration that its current nuisance requirement has been solved.
- **Change the candidate to remove its varying denominator.** In a revised prospective design, replace day-specific `K_t` normalization by a single positive training-only constant, and place all newly admitted post-cutoff history in the shared baseline under one fixed nuisance specification. The candidate then has no day-varying `K_t` argument. Test that property algebraically and synthetically before any historical measurement. This changes the proposed feature and cannot be adopted as a quiet implementation detail. The resulting claim remains relative to the declared benchmark, with no general causal identification guarantee.

A finite matched model can support a carefully qualified predictive comparison. It cannot certify that any gain arises exclusively from the current observation without additional assumptions on the prior-information response surface. Do not choose the nuisance basis, a denominator, normalization scale, duration treatment or interpretation by comparing resulting empirical scores.
