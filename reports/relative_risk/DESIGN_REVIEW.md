# Independent prospective measurement/design review

**Pre-numeric review; no new empirical measurements, targets, correlations, associations, or fits.** The bounded question is defensible as **QQQ ETF versus SPX price-index relative intraday OHLC risk**, subject to the source/calendar audit and a frozen measurement gate. It is not an exact Nasdaq-100-versus-SPX index experiment. The proposed baseline has **27 columns**: intercept; 18 risk/leverage histories across both assets; four IV controls; and four weekday indicators. The single correlation addition makes **28 columns**.

## Target and measurement gate

Write raw `G[a,s] = 0.5 log(H/L)^2 − (2 log(2)−1) log(C/O)^2`, with the already proposed floor `ε=1e−10`. The response is fixed as `log(max(G_QQQ,ε)) − log(max(G_SPX,ε))` on the same next observed SPX session. This is a conditional mean of a log proxy ratio under squared loss; neither exponentiation nor a different label name turns it into a ratio of expected variances, portfolio variance, or tradable spread. A zero or negative log ratio is valid; its sign must not be repaired or flipped.

The no-floor-dependence rule is reasonable as a conservative **measurement admission rule**, not a predictive selection test. Define activation explicitly before seeing values; I recommend **raw `G <= ε`, including equality**, with each asset checked separately. The eligibility pool must be determined from source dates and declared fences before any correlation validity, feature completeness, target value, or score filter. Otherwise a numerical exclusion could conceal a floor hit.

The strongest simple version is to require `G > ε` for every finite observed asset record on the full bounded SPX reference calendar. This covers predictor histories as well as all date-supported training/scored labels and avoids circular pool selection. A narrower date-supported training/target pool is defensible only if its exact date-only construction is frozen first. Whichever scope is selected, any hit means **measurement-insufficient for this proposed design**: retain the audit and stop, without deleting the row, changing the floor, changing orientation, winsorizing the response, or choosing a substitute target. Missing fields, nonfinite values, and malformed OHLC ranges need separately declared counts/statuses; none may be silently recoded as a floor hit or removed after viewing its value.

The gate addresses a real log-ratio problem: if both sides bind, the floor can manufacture a zero response; if one binds, the floor can determine its magnitude. With valid positive OHLC ranges, `|log(C/O)| <= log(H/L)`, so algebraically `G >= (1.5−2 log(2)) log(H/L)^2 >= 0`. Material negative values therefore call for source/range validation. Very small or zero ranges remain possible and must follow the frozen gate rather than an outcome-based repair.

Passing the gate would only establish independence from this floor on its declared scope. Range-based proxy error, differing ETF/index construction and opening-price conventions, intraday jumps, archival revisions, and discrete observation still remain. The underlying Garman–Klass paper derives its estimator under a maintained diffusion model and discusses limitations of discrete monitoring; it does not establish that these vendor daily ranges are measured integrated variance. [Garman and Klass, original paper, equation (19a) and structural-model discussion](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/fx/files/a_estimation_of_security_price.pdf)

## Calendar and correlation

Preserve the bounded observed SPX reference calendar and align QQQ by exact civil date. Do not intersect away holes before rolling. The target requires both assets on the same reference next-session date; the next common date is not a substitute. For previous-close and overnight histories, verify that the preceding QQQ source session equals the preceding reference session. An extra or missing source date must not create an unnoticed longer return. The intraday target itself needs only the target session's OHLC, but baseline total-risk and leverage histories still require the declared previous-close alignment.

The sole increment is strict trailing-22 Pearson correlation of the paired raw open-to-close log returns, ending at the previous reference session. Use all 22 aligned pairs with centered arithmetic and no pairwise deletion. A zero denominator is unknown, not zero correlation. Exact correlations of ±1 are valid; numerical out-of-range handling must be specified before derivation, with no silent clipping to an interior bound or alternate transform. All arms, including the historical mean, share the resulting common rows. A zero training scale, fewer than 1,000 mature common rows, or failed fixed fit ends the study under its registered rules.

Market features stop one session before entry; labels mature at the target session's close. Monthly refit dates depend on complete current features before future label readiness is inspected. Existing phase and sealed-source fences remain binding. A no-floor gate using the bounded historical outcomes is a predeclared retrospective measurement screen; a passing study must not describe that gate as an online trading decision or pristine untouched validation.

## Ridge identity and information claim

For one shared training design `X` including the intercept, fixed training-only scaling, penalty matrix `P=diag(0,1,…,1)`, and `α=.01`, ridge is linear in the target: `β(y)=(X'X/n+αP)⁻¹X'y/n`. Consequently `β(y_Q−y_S)=β(y_Q)−β(y_S)`, and the same identity holds on every common application design. Train means/intercepts also subtract exactly. This is an algebraic identity; no empirical fit was used here.

A meaningful prewritten synthetic test should compare both coefficients and application predictions for two arbitrary component responses and their difference, with correlated regressors, a nonzero intercept, identical rows/scales, and the same mean-MSE penalty convention. It should also cover translation of the component responses and exclude application rows from scaling. Different masks, weights, penalties, target-dependent selection, clipping, or nonlinear postprocessing void the shared-operator argument. Subtracting two otherwise identical component ridge forecasts is therefore not a second model arm.

Likewise, adding a linear log-risk or log-IV ratio when its two components are already columns adds no information and can merely change ridge penalization. The proposed correlation is a joint statistic not determined by the retained marginal summaries, but it uses existing raw inputs and its predictive value is unknown. Only that one augmentation versus the strong baseline and versus the same-row mean should enter the later protocol; neither individual coefficients nor newly chosen subperiods can supply extra discoveries. Source and measurement feasibility must be decided before that forecast run is authorized. No earlier artifact was changed.
