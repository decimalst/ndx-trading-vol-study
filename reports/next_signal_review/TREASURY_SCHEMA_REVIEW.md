# Treasury auction schema review

Documentation review for a possible independent source parser covering auction results dated 2010 through 2025-10-20. **The economic definitions are sufficiently clear to specify a parser contract; the exact machine-field mapping and historical formatting remain unverified.** This note registers no experiment and adds no comparisons. No auction-results URL or historical-data endpoint was opened or downloaded. Incidental result snippets returned by documentation searches were excluded from the evidence and no auction values are reproduced here. All substantive evidence below comes from official documentation and index metadata.

## Required quantities and units

| Quantity | Definition and normalized representation | Parser consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Dealer Accepted | Competitive awards to primary dealers bidding for their **own proprietary accounts**, not all bids routed through those dealers. Normalize as par amount in U.S. dollars. Treasury distinguishes own-account primary dealers, own-account non-primary direct bidders, and customers using intermediaries. [Auction FAQ](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/help-center/faqs/auction-faqs/) | Do not label this dealer inventory, net purchases, all dealer intermediation, or an identified demand shortfall. Do not add customer/indirect awards to it. |
| Total Competitive Accepted / Competitive Accepted | Par amount awarded to competitive bids. The intended check is `primary_dealer_accepted + direct_bidder_accepted + indirect_bidder_accepted = competitive_accepted`, using the three mutually exclusive result categories. The source must explicitly establish that its field maps to this total. [Auction categories](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/help-center/faqs/auction-faqs/), [award process](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-356/subpart-C/section-356.20) | The denominator for dealer competitive share is this accepted amount, **not Total Accepted, public offering size, or Competitive Tendered**. Share is dimensionless in [0,1], provided the competitive denominator is positive. |
| Bid-to-Cover Ratio | Total par bids divided by total par awards, **excluding the Federal Reserve's own account**. It includes public noncompetitive demand, so it is not competitive tendered divided by competitive accepted. The exclusion is explicit in the [2002 final rule](https://treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/11-12-2002-final-rule.pdf) and current [31 CFR 356.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-356/subpart-A/section-356.2). | A dimensionless ratio, not a percentage. Preserve the published ratio and its precision. Validate against public tendered/public accepted only after the source's subtotal/SOMA/FIMA inclusion rules are fixed. Do not recompute it with offering amount: award rounding can make that denominator differ. |
| High Yield for nominal notes/bonds | Highest accepted competitive yield; the uniform award price corresponds to it. Yield is the annualized return to maturity, expressed in percentage points. Coupon/Interest Rate is a different field. [Yield definition](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-356/subpart-A/section-356.2), [single-price awards](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-356/subpart-C/section-356.20) | Preserve an explicitly named percentage-point value, optionally a separate decimal-rate conversion. Never infer the scale from observed magnitudes. Do not use average/median yield, coupon, price, or investment rate as an alias. |
| Bill, TIPS, FRN return fields | Bills are bid on discount rate; notes/bonds on yield; TIPS on real yield; FRNs on discount margin. TIPS real yields and FRN discount margins may be negative; nominal note/bond yields and bill discount rates may be zero. [Treasury's November 2024 rules workshop, slides 11–12](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/treasury-auction-rules.pdf) | Require explicit security class and TIPS/floating-rate flags. A single generic `yield` column across these classes would mix different quantities. A nominal fixed-rate study should reject other classes by identity before reading its numerical fields. |
| Par amount versus purchase price | Par amount is the stated principal value at original issuance. Settlement adds/subtracts price premium/discount and accrued interest; TIPS can add inflation adjustment. Price is quoted per $100 par. [31 CFR 356.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-356/subpart-A/section-356.2) | Accepted amounts are principal allocations, not settlement cash. Monetary parser outputs must have an explicit base-dollar scale; never assume a chart shown in millions/billions has the same unit as an API/export. |

The official [query metadata](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/auction-query/) lists separate fields for Competitive Accepted, Competitive Tendered, Competitive Tenders Accepted, Primary Dealer Accepted, Direct Bidder Accepted, Indirect Bidder Accepted, Noncompetitive Accepted, FIMA Noncompetitive Accepted, SOMA Accepted, Total Accepted, and Offering Amount. Similar wording is not permission for fuzzy matching. In particular, `Competitive Tenders Accepted` must not be assumed to be the monetary `Competitive Accepted` field. Its exact data type/meaning remains to be bound from source metadata.

## SOMA and FIMA accounting

The New York Fed's [dated March 20, 2019 rollover FAQ](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/treasury-rollover-faq-190320) explains that SOMA rollovers use noncompetitive bids, receive the auction's stop-out rate, and increase the issue size by the SOMA award. They do not set the competitive stop-out yield. Their size also follows policy and maturing holdings, so changing SOMA amounts must not mechanically change a proposed dealer competitive share.

Store `soma_tendered`, `soma_accepted`, `soma_included`, `fima_included`, and separately identified public noncompetitive/FIMA noncompetitive quantities when available. Do not use a Boolean “included” field as an amount, infer that missing SOMA means zero, or add SOMA to the competitive denominator. The exact meaning of the source's `SOMA Included` and `FIMA Included` flags must be authenticated before applying a total/offering reconciliation.

Conditional accounting checks after the source mapping is established:

- Competitive awards equal the three competitive bidder-category awards.
- Public accepted excludes SOMA. Where a source explicitly defines Total Accepted as public plus SOMA, public accepted equals Total Accepted minus SOMA Accepted.
- Public tendered/public accepted reproduces the published bid-to-cover ratio within its documented rounding precision. Reject ambiguity rather than guessing whether a noncompetitive subtotal already includes FIMA.
- FIMA competitive bids placed through the Federal Reserve Bank of New York are **customer/indirect** bids, not the Federal Reserve's own-account SOMA bids. Foreign versus domestic identity is not established by the direct/indirect categories. [Treasury FAQ](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/help-center/faqs/auction-faqs/)

The presently served undated rollover URL is effective October 29, 2025, after this research ceiling. Its later policy directive must not be applied retrospectively; this note instead uses the dated 2019 documentation for the accounting explanation. This is not a complete reconstruction of every FOMC rollover-policy regime in 2010–2025.

## Reopenings and original-term identity

Treasury defines a reopening as additional issuance of an outstanding security. It retains the original maturity and coupon rate/spread, but has a different issue date and usually a different price. See [Treasury reopenings](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/reopenings/). Thus current remaining term is not the original tenor: a reopened ten-year note must not become a separate “nine-year” family because the displayed remaining term is shorter.

Preserve CUSIP, auction date, issue date, maturity date, reopening flag, Original CUSIP, Original Issue Date, Original Dated Date, Original Security Term, and the supplied current Security Term. Classify from the original-term identity and security type/flags, not rounded `(maturity − issue)` or `(maturity − auction)` days. Verify the original-link relationship against authenticated identity metadata; do not silently infer it from a matching maturity. A CUSIP can appear in multiple auction events, so CUSIP alone is not a unique event key. The proposed event identity is `(CUSIP, auction_date, issue_date)` plus the source's event/document identifier where provided; separate versions of the same event require explicit revision handling.

Unscheduled reopenings make announcement identity particularly important. Treasury's [2022 amendment](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/uoc-noncomp-increase.pdf) clarifies that it does not conduct unscheduled TIPS reopenings. The current schedule is not a historical calendar and must not be used to manufacture absent auctions or classify old events.

## Changes relevant to 2010–2025-10-20

| Date or period | Documented change | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Before the window: April 2008 | The [auction-query index](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/auction-query/) says additional fields were added for all security types. | A 2010 floor avoids assuming those fields existed in the earlier database, but does not prove complete coverage after 2010. |
| Before the window: June 2009 | The [UOC amendment](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/e9-12787.pdf) removed the old guaranteed-bid provision associated with multiple-price auctions and clarified nominal yields/rates may be zero. | Do not extend current dealer/customer classifications backward over this boundary without a separate treatment. The 2010 floor should remain explicit. |
| April 1, 2011 | A [dated final rule](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/fruoc-min-int-rate-03-01-2011.pdf) established a minimum coupon interest rate of 1/8 percent for new note/bond issues. | This is a coupon rule, not a minimum accepted yield. Preserve reopening/new-issue distinctions. |
| July 2013 regulation | Treasury added the auction/issuance framework for [floating-rate notes](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/2013-18178.pdf). The [amendment index](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/) also records later corrections. | New security class and discount-margin/spread fields; do not pool them with nominal fixed-rate yields. This note does not assert an exact first auction from numerical records. |
| July 2016 | The [official amendment index](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/) records non-substantive technical corrections. | Record the documentation version; this is not evidence of a changed dealer-share definition. |
| August 8, 2022 | The [final rule](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/uoc-noncomp-increase.pdf) doubled the noncompetitive per-bidder limit from $5 million to $10 million; clarified customer identification for guarantee arrangements; clarified unscheduled TIPS reopenings and other terminology/calculation points. | Public noncompetitive participation can change, affecting bid-to-cover composition. A bid fulfilling a guarantee to a customer at the auction price is that customer's bid. Preserve this dated reporting clarification; do not assume a numerical series break or “correct” old category totals. |
| Current documentation versus historical releases | Current API documentation has moved, current FAQs mention the Debt Financing System, while the dated 2024 workshop used TAAPS. | Treat these as interface/documentation-version differences. An effective system migration date or complete historical schema-change log was not authenticated here. No inference that own-account category semantics changed is made. |

This bounded review does not certify the absence of other reporting corrections, auction-format changes, or category-description changes within the window. Dated result-format/schema versions and correction notices still need to be pinned during source admission.

## Contract required before an independent source parser

1. **Bind the source interface first.** TreasuryDirect's [developer page](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/legal-information/developers/) now links to an [API-community documentation page](https://api-community.fiscal.treasury.gov/s/communityapi/a01Qo00000pDeK7IAK/enterprise-apisustreasurymarketablesecuritiesexperienceapi), which rendered only a CSS error during this review. The Fiscal Data dataset's public dictionary landing page was identifiable but direct retrieval returned 403; a metadata-only RAML lookup did not resolve. Consequently, exact provider field spellings, unit multipliers, null tokens, revision timestamp meanings, and API version are **not yet authenticated**. Do not inherit third-party client definitions as authoritative. No account, credentials, or provider contact was requested.
2. **Pin literal mappings and units.** Use a versioned mapping from exact provider keys/tags or document labels to normalized internal names. Proposed internal names include `primary_dealer_accepted_par_usd`, `competitive_accepted_par_usd`, `direct_accepted_par_usd`, `indirect_accepted_par_usd`, `bid_to_cover`, `high_yield_pct`, and the identity fields above. These are proposed parser outputs, not claims about provider key names. Keep original text and source hash beside normalized values.
3. **Separate metadata admission from numbers.** Check raw-byte hash, official source identity, result-versus-announcement status, event dates, class/tenor, and the 2025-10-20 auction/result-publication ceiling before numeric decoding. Known future issue and maturity dates are legitimate descriptors; they are not future observed outcomes and must not be rejected solely for exceeding the ceiling. Future auction results must be excluded and accounted for before values are read.
4. **Parse amounts exactly.** Use decimal arithmetic with an explicitly bound base-dollar scale. Reject nonfinite/negative accepted or tendered amounts, Boolean coercions, malformed grouping, and missing required monetary fields. Preserve actual zeros; do not coerce blanks, dashes, or absent keys to zero. Require positive competitive denominator, bounded dealer share, accepted no greater than tendered, and the accounting checks above. Rounded published ratios need a declared tolerance based on displayed/source precision, not a fitted tolerance.
5. **Preserve events and revisions.** Retain reopening identity, raw document filename/URL, source publication date/time if provided, retrieval time, hash, and any update/revision metadata. Repeated CUSIPs are valid; conflicting versions of the same event are not interchangeable. A current API capture is not automatically the original release vintage. `record_date` is described by Fiscal Data as a publication date; it must not be promoted to an intraday timestamp or presumed first-release date without stronger evidence. [Dataset dictionary landing page](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-securities-auctions-data/)
6. **Prewrite generated tests before any result-body admission.** Cover dealer/customer swaps, competitive-versus-total denominator confusion, SOMA add-ons, separate FIMA treatment, coupon/yield substitutions, percentage-point scaling, missing-versus-zero amounts, reopening CUSIP reuse, shortened remaining terms, conflicting revisions, rounded bid-to-cover, future results, and permitted future maturity dates. Independent parsing should reconstruct the accounting from the pinned raw source without calling the producer parser.

The viable next step is narrowly scoped source-schema authentication and generated parser tests. The rules support a competitive dealer-allocation measure; they do not yet establish a complete historical feed, original-release availability, or predictive value.
