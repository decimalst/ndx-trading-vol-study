# Independent information-channel gap review

Reviewed 2026-09-08. **Primary recommendation: investigate first-released Treasury auction dealer allocations as one new demand/intermediation information channel. Advance a bounded source-admission audit before considering a predictive trial.** No source, feature, comparison, or model is registered by this review.

The proposed question is whether unusually high primary-dealer acceptance at nominal coupon auctions predicts the next five QQQ sessions' variance after controlling for ordinary equity volatility, Treasury-market repricing, auction timing, supply, and aggregate auction demand. This is a hypothesis, not a finding. A large dealer share may reflect weak customer demand, dealer preferences, or bidder composition; it does not directly measure dealer balance-sheet distress or forced inventory retention.

## Free source and a defensible clock

TreasuryDirect explicitly provides historical auction announcements and results press releases; non-TIPS releases are available from July 27, 1998. Its annual archive is the proposed source of dated documents and offering/result identities. The current rolling results page or a today's-history query alone would not establish original vintages. [TreasuryDirect announcement/results archive](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/announcements-data-results/announcement-results-press-releases/).

Treasury's own chronology says that separate direct-bidder, indirect-bidder, and primary-dealer competitive statistics began in May 2003, initially about 20 minutes after the main release. By September 2004 those statistics became available to the press at the same time as the results. This establishes a historical publication process for the required category data; it does not prove that every currently hosted document is an unchanged original. [Treasury auction timeline](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/research-center/timeline/auctions/).

A June 1, 2009 rule removed a guaranteed-bid provision that had treated certain customer commitments as dealer bids and required participants to reconsider proper bidder identification. Use **2010-01-01 as a prospective source floor**, including all rolling-history inputs, rather than splice across that convention. This calendar boundary is selected from documentation, not from prices or measured predictive performance. [June 2009 original Treasury rule, pages 26085–26086](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/files/laws-and-regulations/auction-regulations-uoc/e9-12787.pdf).

The New York Fed's archived January 2010 policy required primary dealers to participate in Treasury auctions and bid at least their pro-rata share at reasonable prices. That gives dealer take-down a plausible intermediation interpretation, but an obligation to bid is not an obligation to retain every accepted security. A successful forecast would remain a conditional statistical finding. [Archived New York Fed dealer policy](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/pridealers_policies_100111.html).

Use each authenticated result, including its bidder-category addendum, only at the first observed QQQ close **strictly after its actual public-release date**. If the category addendum was later than the headline release, its later date governs. Keep auction date, announcement date, result/addendum release date, issue/settlement date, and current retrieval timestamp separate. Neither a scheduled competitive closing time nor a filename date alone proves actual publication. The one-session delay avoids an intraday execution claim; it does not fix retrospective corrections.

Before admission, pin the annual index, original offering notice, result/addendum, any explicitly linked correction, and exact document bytes. Reconcile identity by auction date plus CUSIP and security type; the same CUSIP can appear in multiple reopenings. A missing/ambiguous original or unresolved correction remains unknown. Do not replace an unavailable first report with a revised current query value. No auction-result body or numerical auction table was acquired in this review, so original-release completeness and correction coverage are still unverified.

## One proposed candidate and its matched comparison

Use regular nominal fixed-rate coupon auctions with original terms **2, 3, 5, 7, 10, and 30 years**, including their reopenings. Pool them under one coefficient; do not screen tenors separately. Exclude bills, TIPS, FRNs, and other terms prospectively. Determine original term from authenticated offering/CUSIP lineage, not a reopened security's shortened remaining-life label. The post-2009 source floor also avoids the transition to the expanded 2009 coupon schedule documented in the Treasury timeline.

For auction `j`, define `s[j]` as primary-dealer competitive accepted amount divided by total competitive accepted amount. The denominator must reconcile with the three accepted bidder categories; exclude noncompetitive and SOMA additions. A true zero dealer amount is valid; an unknown numerator, nonpositive denominator, conflicting category total, or missing document is not zero. Define `m[j]` as the arithmetic mean of `s` over the **12 preceding eligible auctions of the same original term**, requiring complete original-report history. This is a within-term reference level, not a survey expectation.

At origin `t`, let `J[t]` contain reports becoming newly eligible under the preceding clock. The single candidate is `x[t] = sum(s[j] - m[j] for j in J[t])`. On a day with a completely audited absence of a new eligible auction, `x[t]=0`. Do not carry a report forward indefinitely. If any expected result is unknown, the day's candidate and matched rows are unknown. Sum all same-day eligible reports under the same rule rather than select one after inspecting its allocation. This fixed pulse asks about delayed post-release information, not an auction-time trading reaction.

The substantive control should retain the established 12 market inputs and add: separate delayed TLT return, squared-return, log-RV5 and log-RV22 controls; entry weekdays; number of newly eligible auctions; five original-term counts with one omitted reference term; reopening count; and, aggregated across the same `J[t]`, announced log offering size, published stop-out yield, published bid-to-cover ratio, and `m[j]`. These controls distinguish allocation from the event calendar, issue mix, supply, auction repricing, overall bidding interest, and the past dealer-share level. All new Treasury/TLT controls use the same conservative availability boundary as the candidate. The existing pooled TLT stress input does not replace its individual matched return/risk histories.

This subtraction is algebraically equivalent to adding the latest dealer share when the control already contains its prior mean. The **new information is the newly published bidder allocation**, not the subtraction or a claim that it is mathematically orthogonal. Do not call the feature a consensus surprise, an auction tail relative to when-issued yield, or a direct inventory measure: no corresponding historical expectation, pre-auction quote archive, or dealer position record has been admitted.

Retain one next-five-session QQQ variance target and common matured training/application rows. If developed, use one candidate-versus-matched contrast and a candidate-versus-established-market comparison under the then-current cumulative family; both would be required for a lead. Do not infer permission for another estimator, horizon, tenor, or lag grid. Exact source ledger, nuisance basis, estimator, coverage rules, and support/inference contract require prewritten tests and a separate prospective freeze.

## What could stop this route

The immediate task is source admission, not another fit. Official archive access is a credible free path, but this review has not authenticated every first report, found all corrections, verified category totals, or measured the admitted auction count. A dated current document is evidence to audit, not automatic proof of an immutable historical vintage. The earlier claims feasibility note already disclosed incidental viewing of one 2024 auction example; this external-data universe must not be described as completely unseen.

Auction pulses are sparse. Daily rows outside auctions and overlapping five-session targets do not create additional independent allocation releases. Before fitting, freeze distinct-auction support requirements by phase and pooled original term, disclose same-day clusters and missing releases, and choose uncertainty that respects serial dependence and overlapping targets. The previous daily observation floors alone cannot certify this design's power. If documentary completeness or prospective auction support fails, report source-ineligible/insufficient rather than extend phases, drop a tenor, carry values forward, or relax gates after inspection.

This recommendation is ranked on novelty, interpretability, and a documented official release route. It asserts no expected improvement. Existing historical partitions remain repeatedly used exploratory data, and all previous 394 Python files, protocols, and results remain unchanged. Only this review file was written; no raw market arrays, new source numerical tables, features, cohorts, fits, scores, or empirical calculations were read or created.
