## Questions to answer for every record

The later documentary review should answer all of the following, with the exact page/section or XML element and retained source hash supporting each answer. A missing answer remains explicit; it must not be inferred from a plausible filename or expected auction schedule.

1. **What event occurred?** Does originating-agency text describe a regular nominal fixed-rate auction, a reopening, a test/exercise, another sale/operation, a cancellation, or a correction/republication of an existing event? Does the body identify the same CUSIP and auction date as the selected archive record? Retain issue date and any explicit event identifier to disambiguate repeated CUSIPs. Verify note/bond class and any inflation/floating-rate designation. A displayed term group alone does not establish eligibility.
2. **What is each document's role?** Classify announcement, competitive result, noncompetitive result, first bidder-category addendum, schedule/terms notice, correction/replacement, test/other notice, or unresolved. Record the event(s) it actually addresses. A shared special notice may legitimately address several auctions. The competitive-result filename prefix `R` is not a revision flag; the special-notice slot does not establish a correction.
3. **What is the original tenor and reopening lineage?** Preserve the supplied remaining term separately from the authenticated original term, original CUSIP/issue identity where present, and reopening status. Can the 9-year/29-year remaining-term records be bound to the original ten-/thirty-year security? If a required original announcement is not in the pilot, identify the precise missing lineage evidence for a bounded follow-up; do not infer the tenor by rounding time to maturity or silently fetch an invented URL.
4. **When was the required information released?** Distinguish the document's public-release date/time, the auction date/time, announcement date, issue date, maturity date and a revision date. Does an explicit dated release header or other dated originating-agency evidence support public availability of this particular version? Preserve raw wording and timezone when supplied. If PDF and XML expose different kinds of dates, explain those roles before comparing them. Neither the index's auction date nor a file creation/update date can silently become the first publication clock.
5. **Where did the bidder allocations first appear?** Does the initial competitive result contain all three accepted bidder categories and their competitive total, or is a separate category addendum required? If separate, does it identify the same event and have its own supported first-release date? An initially later addendum is not automatically a correction. The official timeline's general statement about simultaneous category releases after September 2004 does not establish the contents or dates of a particular captured document. [Treasury auction timeline](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/research-center/timeline/auctions/).
6. **Was anything subsequently changed?** Does a linked notice expressly amend the schedule, offering terms, event identity, competitive result or category figures; cancel or replace a document; or describe a test? Identify the affected original, changed fields, notice release evidence, and whether the original remains recoverable. Classify an announcement change separately from a correction to realized auction results. A special notice linked after the auction deserves inspection, but the timing of its filename is not proof of a revision.
7. **Do the literal schemas support the intended quantities?** Locate accepted versus tendered amounts, own-account primary/direct and customer/indirect accepted categories, competitive subtotal, noncompetitive/FIMA/SOMA quantities and inclusion flags, offering amount, published bid-to-cover and nominal high yield. Record exact labels/tags, units, null notation and stated precision before numerical conversion. Keep coupon, price, discount rate and floating-rate discount margin distinct. A noncompetitive-results document cannot supply the competitive dealer-allocation denominator.
8. **Do the two formats describe the same version?** Independently decode the announcement PDF/XML pair and competitive-result PDF/XML pair under the tested mapping. Compare named identity and release fields first; only after schema admission compare numerical quantities semantically in explicit units. Explain absent fields, a separate addendum, date-role differences and actual contradictions individually. Agreement is a format/extraction check on two official representations, not an independent historical publication timestamp or proof of immutability.

The evidence ledger should retain one association for every selected record/document role, even when shared URLs are captured only once. For each unique document preserve requested/effective URL, HTTP/content-type evidence, capture time, raw-byte hash, extracted-text hash where applicable, document role, body identity, supported release clock, version relationship and precise evidence locations. For each event preserve the conclusions and unresolved questions above. Keep original text beside normalized metadata; do not overwrite the original when resolving a conflict.

## Release clocks, corrections and the ceiling

For a proposed first-report dealer-allocation feature, establish when every required first-released field was publicly available. If the bidder categories first appear in a later addendum, use that supported availability date; a main result's earlier date cannot make the later categories known. Once documentary admission succeeds, eligibility starts at the first QQQ session strictly after the latest required first-release date. Do not infer a tradable intraday timestamp from the official timeline, auction time or a date-only header.

Preserve a recoverable original alongside a dated correction. The proposed first-report feature retains original amounts and its original-history references; a later correction is an audit event, not permission to backfill the past. If only a demonstrated corrected version survives and the original cannot be recovered, the corresponding first-report inputs remain unknown. A later schedule notice that changes no result field does not automatically move every result's availability date.

For any linked or cross-referenced notice with an apparent filename date after 2025-10-20, flag `apparent_post_ceiling_filename` before numerical decoding. That flag authenticates neither the body date nor the correction relationship. If the primary release date is actually after the ceiling, retain it as later audit evidence where review is authorized, but do not admit its numbers, reconstruct a missing pre-ceiling original from its corrected values, or use its later knowledge as a historical model input. If it demonstrates that an earlier result was corrected, record that source-quality finding and apply the original-recovery rule. If date evidence is unresolved, do not guess a pre-ceiling date. Conversely, a maturity or issue date after the ceiling can be an already announced descriptor and is not by itself a future-result violation.

Today's retrieval time, HTTP Last-Modified, PDF creation metadata and current index update fields are separate evidence fields, not substitutes for the supported release date. Dated primary release content, coherent identity and a resolved review of known relevant notices can support qualified exploratory use without a contemporaneous cryptographic hash. Generic absence of a version API is an archival limitation, not a reason to mark every row unavailable. The pilot does not claim a complete known-correction review for the remaining history.

## PDF/XML and eventual numerical admission

Require literal, versioned mappings for the two formats. Compare CUSIP, auction/issue/maturity dates, security type, current/original term where supplied, reopening identity and separately named release dates before amounts. Formatting differences, whitespace, commas and different display precision are not conflicting economics. Compare amounts with exact decimal conversion into declared base-dollar units; compare published ratio/yield values using declared source/display precision, never a tolerance chosen after an observed mismatch. Do not collapse blank/absent into zero, or a percentage-point yield into a decimal rate without recording the conversion.

The prospective numerical checks remain those in the schema review: accepted own-account primary/direct plus customer/indirect categories equal competitive accepted; the competitive denominator excludes SOMA and public noncompetitive awards; accepted and tendered are not interchangeable; and public bid-to-cover accounting uses authenticated subtotal/inclusion rules. Preserve the published bid-to-cover rather than deriving it from offering size. A full PDF/XML match cannot compensate for an unresolved common source date or a demonstrated overwritten original. A mismatch must produce a field-specific unresolved finding; neither format automatically wins.

Before applying a new parser mapping, generated tests should cover at least: reused CUSIP across distinct auctions; a test event with valid-looking result labels; correction versus first addendum; later addendum availability; post-ceiling correction to an earlier auction; permitted future maturity; shared notices with different event applicability; the observed unequal filename suffix pattern; announcement/result substitution; PDF/XML identity or unit conflict; original-tenor versus remaining-term confusion; missing versus zero amounts; dealer/customer swaps; competitive/total/tendered substitutions; and separate FIMA/SOMA accounting. These are prospective requirements for the implementation owner, not tests executed by this documentary review.

The pilot outcome should account for all 22 event rows and all 118 associations with a supported classification or explicit unresolved question. Publish concrete schema/date/identity problems and precise required follow-ups. A successful pilot authorizes a controlled next source-validation step under the parent's gates; it does not register a comparison, select a model, certify full historical coverage, or establish a predictive signal.
